I flew into Bozeman a day ahead of Thorsten Overgaard's workshop, four days of shooting and critique I'd been looking forward to for months. What I didn't expect was for the best story of the trip to happen before the workshop even started.

Earlier that day, getting coffee at Nexus Drive-Up right outside the Sapphire Motel, Austin, who turned out to own the place, clocked the Leicas around our necks before either of us said a word. Thorsten mentioned we were in town a day early, and Austin started listing off places worth the drive. Livingston topped the list.

![The Sapphire Motel sign outside our hotel in Bozeman](./01-sapphire-sign.jpg)
*Our hotel that week, the Sapphire Motel, Bozeman, lit up as we headed out for the evening. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/2.8.*

We found Yellowstone Hat Co first, and Thorsten stood at the window longer than the hats really warranted, working out which one I needed. He wanted to buy me one right there, and it had to be black, he said, or I'd never fit the scene.

![Thorsten looking through the window of Yellowstone Hat Co, Livingston](./02-hat-shop.jpg)
*Thorsten, deciding I needed a Stetson. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

A block over he found something else to lean against, an old International truck bed with the name still legible under the rust, and pulled out the lens he'd been wanting to show me all morning, a new Leitz Hektor 73mm. He struck the pose without being asked.

![Thorsten leaning against an old International truck bed in Livingston, holding his new Leitz Hektor 73mm lens](./03-thorsten-truck.jpg)
*Thorsten, showing off his new Leitz Hektor 73mm, still leaning on somebody else's truck. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/4.8.*

Livingston runs thick with galleries for a town its size, though nothing about the afternoon suggested anything more organized than an ordinary Friday. Thorsten kept circling back to the thing he's [spent years writing about](https://www.overgaard.dk/): find the beautiful light and the rest takes care of itself. By early evening Main Street had it, gold and long-shadowed, a storm building over the mountains behind the storefronts.

![Main Street, Livingston, gold evening light and mountains behind](./04-main-street.jpg)
*Main Street, Livingston, and a storm building over the Absarokas. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/2.4.*

A woman had her easel out two doors down, painting the same block we were photographing, working in oil what we were working in light. Neither of us said anything to her. There didn't seem to be anything to add.

![A woman painting at an easel on the street in Livingston](./05-painter.jpg)
*Working the same street, different tools. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

By the time we thought about the rodeo back in Bozeman, the light was gone. We spent the better part of an hour searching our phones for a ride. The first number we found belonged to a driver who told us he didn't run that late to Bozeman anymore, but gave us two other numbers before he hung up. One went to voicemail. The other was Jason, who was about to throw meat on his grill for dinner and came anyway, because he couldn't stand the thought of us stuck out there. The drive back took maybe twenty-five minutes.

We got to the fairgrounds as the rodeo was already breaking up, riders and stock trucks pulling out, the crowd still in their good hats and pressed shirts.

![The grandstand emptying out at the fairgrounds in Bozeman](./06-grandstand.jpg)
*The grandstand emptying under a sky that hadn't decided what it was doing. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

We were too busy photographing the ones leaving to talk to any of them.

![Two riders leaving the fairgrounds after the rodeo ended](./07-two-riders.jpg)
*Two riders leaving after it was already over. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

![A rider still on the grounds, unhurried](./08-still-there.jpg)
*Still in no hurry to leave. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

![The last rider to leave the fairgrounds, backlit](./09-last-one-out.jpg)
*The last one out, backlit and unbothered. Leica M10-R, Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 Pre-ASPH, f/1.4.*

We missed the rodeo. The tip from Austin, and the ride from Jason, who didn't have to give it, were the better story anyway. The black hat turned into a running joke for the rest of the workshop, Thorsten bringing it up almost daily, until I finally ordered one online after I got home. What four days with him did to how I see a street is its own story, next time.
